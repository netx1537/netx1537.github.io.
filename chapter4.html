Perceptual adaptation is the brain's mechanism for adjusting to prolonged sensory input, enabling consistent, stable perception of the environment despite changing conditions. It filters out constant, irrelevant stimuli—such as background noise or smells—to focus on new information. This process increases neural efficiency and creates aftereffects, where subsequent stimuli are interpreted based on previous exposure. Perceptual adaptation shows that senses do not provide a full, unchanging picture of reality, but rather a "sketch" that is heavily processed and adjusted by the brain.
